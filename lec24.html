Lecture 24 MED priority

Resource Management I — Green Cloud & VM Consolidation

Prof. Soumya Kanti Ghosh · Dept. of Computer Science and Engineering, IIT Kharagpur

Introduces cloud resource management: resource types, data center power economics, and the green cloud framework. Covers VM consolidation via live migration (with a power-consumption example), VM image minimization, and research directions. Conceptual with one illustrative numerical example; no exam-style worked problems.

1 · Why Resource Management?
Provider perspective
Finite resources must serve as many consumers as possible while maximizing profit without SLA violations. Even with budget, physical power supply from the grid can be a hard ceiling.
Consumer perspective
Cloud appears as an infinite, elastic pool accessed on a pay-as-you-go basis. The consumer should receive the requested resources without caring how they are physically managed.
Environmental obligation
Carbon footprint and energy consumption are constrained by legal obligations and sustainability targets — not just economics. Energy availability can cap growth regardless of available capital.
Resource management definition: Operations used to control how capabilities provided by cloud resources and services are made available to external entities (users, applications, services) in an efficient way.
Efficient means: maximizing ISP profit, minimizing energy consumption, respecting SLAs, or any combination — the management objective is configurable.

Logical resources
ApplicationsCommunication apps, business software
ProcessesCPU executions of user workloads
MonitoringTools that observe physical resources
ManagementOrchestration and control-plane services
Layered view: IaaS (infrastructure) is the primary focus of resource management, but PaaS and SaaS layers also have resources that must be managed. Customers access cloud via heterogeneous endpoints: servers, laptops, smartphones, tablets.
3 · Data Center Power Consumption
Scale: Servers consume ~0.5% of the world's total electricity. When data center logistics (cooling, UPS, AC) are included, the fraction rises above 1%. Energy demand for servers is reportedly doubling every 5–6 years.
Compute infrastructure
Servers, storage, network
Primary workload carriers. Power use is roughly proportional to active cores and memory, but not linearly — power consumption saturates as core count grows (non-linear relationship exploited by consolidation).
Environmental infrastructure
Power supply, cooling, logistics
UPS, AC units, rack logistics. Power draw is approximately equal to compute infrastructure — the "overhead" cost of keeping servers alive is nearly as large as the compute cost itself.
Grid ceiling: Even with unlimited budget, the local electricity grid may not supply additional power. A university data center cannot simply double capacity by paying more if the utility has no surplus.
4 · Green Cloud Framework
VM controls (compute side)
SchedulingPower-aware and thermal-aware VM placement
VM imagesMinimized, service-oriented OS images
MigrationLive VM migration to consolidate onto fewer nodes
ShutdownDynamic hibernation of idle servers
Data center design (infra side)
Rack designPhysical layout to minimize heat paths
CoolingTargeted rack-level cooling enclosures, not room-wide
RecirculationManaging hot aisle / cold aisle air flow
IntegrationIntegrated server + rack + cooling systems
Combined impact: No single technique is sufficient. Power-aware scheduling + VM image minimization + rack-level cooling together produce greater savings than any one implemented alone.
5 · VM Consolidation via Live Migration
Observation: Power vs. active processors is non-linear — power consumption saturates as core count grows. Concentrating VMs on fewer nodes lets idle servers be hibernated, saving their idle power draw.

Power example (greedy consolidation)

ScenarioActive nodesPower calculationTotal power
Distributed (4 nodes, 2 VMs each)44 × 138 W~552 W
Consolidated (1 node, 8 VMs; 3 hibernate)1170 W + 3 × 105 W~485 W
Caveat: Migration itself has a cost (network bandwidth, CPU overhead during transfer). VMs may be of different flavors and cannot always be packed arbitrarily. Greedy algorithms must track available cores per node and check capacity constraints before migrating.

Live migration workflow

Load decreases
Consolidate → hibernate
Monitor load; live-migrate VMs to more utilized nodes using hypervisor live migration support; shut down or hibernate newly empty nodes to eliminate idle power draw.
Load increases
Wake-on-LAN → schedule
Send wake-up signal (Wake-on-LAN) to hibernating nodes; wait for them to come online; schedule new VMs to the freshly available nodes.
6 · VM Image Minimization
Problem: Default VM images (e.g. Ubuntu 9.04 at ~4 GB) include packages and services the consumer never uses. Larger images increase live migration latency and carry idle daemons that waste CPU/memory.
Remove unused packages
Strip X11, window managers, etc.
Eliminate any package not required by the intended service. Service-oriented architecture demands service-oriented VM images — not general-purpose OS installs.
Optimize boot sequence
Read-ahead profiling + reorder
Profile disk access at boot; reorder the sequence to prefetch boot files, minimizing CPU idle time from I/O waits. Reduces boot time and energy consumed during startup.
Custom Linux kernel
Build for DomU (no graphics/sound)
Compile a kernel with only the drivers and modules required. No 3D graphics, no sound subsystem — just compute and network for the target workload.
Results (example)
4 GB → ~600 MB; 38s → 8s boot
At 100 image launches per hour (small cloud) or 1000 per hour (production), the energy savings compound significantly across the fleet.
Impact: Smaller image → faster live migration (less data over network) + lower idle resource consumption + less energy per boot. Scales enormously across thousands of simultaneous VM launches.
Summary
Resource management: Controls how cloud resources are made available to users, applications, and services efficiently — balancing profit, SLA compliance, and energy constraints simultaneously.
Green cloud framework: Two orthogonal axes — VM controls (scheduling, image minimization, live migration) and data center design (rack layout, targeted cooling). Both must be addressed together for meaningful impact.
VM consolidation: Non-linear power-vs-utilization relationship means concentrating VMs on fewer nodes and hibernating idle nodes reduces total power consumption; live migration enables this dynamically.
Research frontier: Power-aware + thermal-aware scheduling, integrated server-rack-cooling strategies, customer profiling for tailored VM images, and next-generation energy-efficient data center design are all open research areas.
