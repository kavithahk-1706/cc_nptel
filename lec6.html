Lecture 06 High priority

Architecture — Deployment Models

Prof. Soumya Kanti Ghosh · Dept. of Computer Science and Engineering, IIT Kharagpur

Covers the four cloud deployment models (public, private, community and hybrid), each with its on-site and outsourced variants. For every model the lecture walks through the same checklist: network dependency, hidden workload locations, multi-tenancy risk, data import/export limits, security, up-front cost and resource availability. Core exam content that sits alongside the service models (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS) from the previous lecture.

1 · Deployment Models: Overview
Besides the service models (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS), a cloud also has a deployment model. All sorts of services can be hosted in the different deployment models.

2 · Public Cloud
Public cloud: cloud infrastructure provisioned for open use by the general public. It may be owned, managed and operated by a business, academic or government organization, or some combination of them. It exists on the premises of the cloud provider (CSP).
Google App Engine Microsoft Windows Azure IBM Smart Cloud Amazon EC2
Key point: an enterprise or individual subscribes over the internet and gets the services. Anyone who can pay can use it, subject to legal and policy conformance. The computing and storage infrastructure resides at the CSP's premises, not at the user's.

Public setting

▶ 02:42
Worth watching: diagram of clients (including possible attackers) reaching a cloud provider over the public internet, with an optional subscriber-controlled security perimeter.
The provider's computing and storage resources are potentially large. Communication links are assumed to be implemented over the public internet. The cloud serves a diverse pool of clients, and not all are faithful: there can be attackers and hackers.

Characteristics and issues

AspectDetail
Workload locations hidden from clientsThe provider may migrate a subscriber's workload or data at any time, to data centres where cost is low, and anywhere unless it offers optional location restriction policies. The user does not know where the VM or data resides, which server or location, or with whom.
Risks from multi-tenancyA single machine may be shared by the workloads of any combination of subscribers, so a workload may be co-resident with that of competitors or adversaries. Introduces both reliability and security risk.
Network dependencySubscribers connect to providers via the public internet. The connection depends on internet infrastructure such as DNS servers, router infrastructure and inter-router links. Network connectivity must be available up to a mark.
Limited visibility and control over dataProvider operation details and software are usually proprietary and not available for examination. A subscriber cannot verify that data has been completely deleted from the provider's systems. The only assurance is an SLA or MOU.
Elasticity: illusion of unlimited resourcesPublic clouds are generally unrestricted in location or size, with flexibility in moving workloads to match available resources. Resources are provisioned when needed and released (de-provisioned) when not; theoretically infinite scaling up and down.
Low up-front costsBuilding your own private cloud means purchasing hardware, housing it, installing and testing software, and maintaining it. In a public cloud you just pay and use.
Restrictive default SLAsDefault service level agreements specify limited promises. Usually the provider's terms must be followed; special rates and SLAs can be negotiated only for large-scale deployments. A small institution or the public at large follows what is provided.
Example: if IIT Kharagpur moved some or all of its labs to a public cloud, overall maintenance cost and load would reduce, but the first dependency is that network connectivity must always be available.
Caveat: if you are not very stringent on legal and policy matters about security, hidden locations are acceptable. Otherwise, multi-tenancy and loss of visibility are real concerns.
3 · Private Cloud
Private cloud: cloud infrastructure provisioned for exclusive use by a single organization comprising multiple consumers (e.g. business units). It may be owned, managed and operated by the organization, a third party, or some combination, and it may exist on or off premises. Example: an IIT KGP private cloud catering to all its departments and sections.
Eucalyptus OpenStack Ubuntu Enterprise Cloud (UEC) Amazon VPC (Virtual Private Cloud) VMware Cloud Infrastructure Suite Microsoft ECI data center
Contrary to popular belief: a private cloud may exist off premises and be managed by a third party. Even then it is under your jurisdiction, policies and organizational rules.

Two private cloud scenarios

4 · On-site Private Cloud
▶ 10:02
Worth watching: diagram of a private cloud inside a subscriber-controlled security perimeter, with a boundary controller, legitimate access path and blocked access.
The security perimeter extends around both the subscriber's on-site resources and the private cloud's resources, so the organization's legal and security control encompasses the private cloud. The perimeter itself does not guarantee control over the private cloud's resources, but the subscriber can exercise control over them.

Issues and characteristics

AspectDetail
Network dependencyDepends on the organization's internet and network.
Subscribers still need IT skillsThe organization maintains its own cloud, so it needs the traditional IT skills to manage user devices plus cloud IT skills.
Workload locations hidden from clientsA private cloud must migrate workloads between machines without inconveniencing clients. The organization chooses the physical infrastructure, but individual clients (e.g. its own subunits) may still not know where their workloads physically exist.
Risks from multi-tenancyWorkloads of different clients may reside concurrently on the same systems and local networks, separated only by access policies implemented by the cloud provider's software. A flaw in the software or policies could expose client workloads to one another.
Data import/export and performance limitsOn-demand bulk data import/export is limited by the on-site cloud's network capacity. Real-time or critical processing may be problematic because of networking limitations.
Potentially strong security from external threatsThe subscriber can implement an appropriately strong security perimeter, to the same level of security achievable for non-cloud resources.
Significant-to-high up-front costsCloud management software must be installed on systems within the organization: numerous commodity systems, or a limited number of high-performance systems for process- or data-intensive workloads. Installing and managing it incurs significant up-front cost, even if the software is free and much of the hardware already exists.
Limited resourcesAt any given time the cloud has a fixed computing and storage capacity, sized to anticipated workloads and cost restrictions. Augmenting means purchasing, installing and interoperating with existing systems.
Security example: IIT Kharagpur developed a private cloud for research purposes, Meghamala, within its own network premises. Whatever network security features IIT Kharagpur has also apply to this infrastructure.
Limited resources example: estimate storage and computing needs and install with some provision, say buying x or installing 1.5x. You are then limited to that 1.5x.
5 · Outsourced Private Cloud
▶ 13:47
Worth watching: diagram of a cloud provider's private cloud on the left, the subscriber on the right, two separate security perimeters, and a boundary controller link between them.
An outsourced private cloud has two security perimeters: one implemented by the cloud subscriber and one by the provider. They are joined by a protected communications link. The security of data and processing in the outsourced private cloud depends on the strength and availability of both perimeters and of the protected link. The infrastructure, the channel and the provider end must all be secured to the expected level.

Issues and characteristics

AspectDetail
Network dependencySubscribers may have an option to provision unique, protected and reliable communication links with the provider.
Workload locations hidden from clientsSame issue as before.
Risks from multi-tenancySame implications as on-site private. Other organizations may also host their private clouds on the same provider.
Data import/export and performance limitsSame as on-site private.
Potentially strong securityStill private and not open to everyone. Some infrastructure may be shared, with possible threat at a lower level, but at the higher level nobody is allowed in.
Modest-to-significant up-front costsResources are provisioned by the provider. Start-up costs for the subscriber relate to negotiating the SLA, possibly upgrading the subscriber's network, switching from traditional to cloud-hosted applications, porting existing non-cloud operations to the cloud, and training.
Extensive resources availableA subscriber can rent resources in any quantity offered by the provider. Provisioning and operating computing equipment at scale is a core competency of providers.
Takeaway: outsourcing keeps the cloud private but shifts maintenance and installation to a third party, so up-front cost and resource limits improve. Both security perimeters and the link now matter.
6 · Community Cloud
Community cloud: cloud infrastructure provisioned for exclusive use by a specific community of consumers from organizations that have shared concerns (e.g. mission, security requirements, policy and compliance considerations). It may be owned, managed and operated by one or more organizations in the community, a third party, or some combination, and it may exist on or off premises. It can operate as public or private.
Google Apps for Government Microsoft Government Community Cloud
Analogy: several organizations A, B, C and X, Y, Z can form different combinations: ABC or XYZ as one community, A with X and Y as another. A community can exist at one point of time and not at another, and an organization can belong to more than one community, like being part of an office group and also a residential community. Grouping organizations with the same type of concern or workflow helps productivity.

On-site community cloud

▶ 17:47
Worth watching: diagram of two groups of organizations, joined through boundary controllers and security perimeters, with some organizations providing cloud services and others consuming them.
Made up of a set of participant organizations. Each participant may provide cloud services, consume them, or both. At least one organization must provide cloud services, and each organization implements a security perimeter. Participants are connected via links between boundary controllers that allow access through their security perimeters (e.g. firewall policies).
AspectDetail
Access policyMay be complex. With N community members, a decision must be made, implicitly or explicitly, on how to share each member's local cloud resources with the others. Leakage of information between communities must be restricted. Policy specification techniques such as role-based access control (RBAC) and attribute-based access control express the sharing policies.
Network dependencyPresent, as in the other deployment models.
Subscribers still need IT skillsProviders in the community need skills similar to on-site private, but the overall configuration may be more complex and need a higher skill level. Identity and access control configuration among participants may be complex.
Workload locations hidden from clientsParticipant organizations providing services may employ an outsourced private cloud as part of their implementation strategy.
Data import/export and performance limitsLinks between participants can be provisioned at various levels of performance, security and reliability. Limitations are similar to the outsourced private cloud. Communities can be loosely coupled, which makes this harder to manage.
Potentially strong securityDepends on the security perimeters of all participating organizations and the strength of the communication links. Essentially similar to outsourced private, but with possibly more links and perimeters. Being in one community gives better resistance to external threats, based on community policies plus your own.
Highly variable up-front costsDepend greatly on whether the organization only consumes or also provides cloud services. A participant that provides services faces costs similar to on-site private (significant-to-high).

7 · Hybrid Cloud
Hybrid cloud: a composition of two or more distinct cloud infrastructures (private, community or public) that remain unique entities but are bound together by standardized or proprietary technology that enables data and application portability.
Windows Azure (capable of hybrid cloud) VMware vCloud (Hybrid Cloud Services)

Why hybrid is important

Usage patterns differ: some usage is more critical or vulnerable to security threats and is kept private, with proper network perimeter security. Other usage need not be so secure and can go public. For example, practice sessions in computing labs need much less security than student records or examination data. The same type of operation can be outsourced to a public cloud, while the other is kept private even if that is less economical.
Sudden demand: increasing resources in a private cloud (provisioning, purchasing) is a long process. You can instead use a public cloud for a short period for as long as the process is in place.
Portability and interoperability: data and sometimes applications must work across clouds. An application running on a private cloud and then provisioned as a VM in the public domain may need resizing, and portability issues must be looked into.

Composition and variation

▶ 25:45
Worth watching: diagram of a hybrid cloud built from on-site private, outsourced private, on-site community, outsourced community and public clouds, all linked together.
A hybrid cloud has significant variations in performance, reliability and security properties, depending on the type of clouds chosen to build it.

Complexity and churn

Extremely complex
An application running over a combination of public and private clouds needs a complex underlying architecture to run seamlessly.
Changes over time
Constituent clouds may join and leave. A public cloud may disconnect when a subscription ends or the pricing model differs, or may cease to exist.
Needs may grow
More public or community clouds may be added when more resources are needed.
Caveat: constituent clouds leaving or joining makes the whole process pretty complex to handle.
8 · Choosing a Deployment Model
Key point: the choice depends entirely on requirements.
Small organization or individual
Public cloud may be a good solution, as long as the business does not have needs that force a move to its own private cloud.
Mission-critical clients
If your clients are in sectors like financial or defense, they may insist you keep resources on your own premises rather than outsourcing or using public clouds.
Mixed needs
Build a hybrid, or classify applications and data into categories: this bunch to private, this to public, this to community.
Example: a data storage provider can keep storage on its own premises, or outsource and provision it from public clouds, depending on what its clients demand.
Challenge: managing all these categories is another big challenge for an organization or institution.
Summary
Deployment models: public, private, community and hybrid; all service models can be hosted on any of them.
Public cloud: open to anyone who can pay, at the CSP's premises. Hidden workload locations, multi-tenancy risk, limited visibility and restrictive default SLAs, but low up-front cost and near-infinite elasticity.
Private cloud: exclusive to one organization, either on-site or outsourced to a hosting company. On-site gives strong security and control but high up-front cost and fixed capacity; outsourced has two security perimeters joined by a protected link, with lower costs and extensive resources.
Community cloud: shared by organizations with common concerns (mission, security, compliance), on-site or outsourced. Access policy is complex and handled with RBAC or attribute-based access control.
Hybrid cloud: two or more distinct clouds bound by technology that enables data and application portability. Useful for mixed security needs and sudden demand, but complex and changes as constituent clouds join and leave.
Examples given: IIT Kharagpur's Meghamala private cloud; practice labs on public cloud versus student records and exams on private; the x vs 1.5x provisioning example for on-site private.
Recurring issues: network dependency, hidden workload locations, multi-tenancy risk, data import/export limits and up-front cost recur across all four models, with differences in degree.
Choice: small organizations may do well on public cloud, mission-critical clients need private, and mixed needs call for hybrid or classifying applications and data.
