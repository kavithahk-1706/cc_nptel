SLA Tutorial — Numerical Problems & Best Practices

Prof. Soumya Kanti Ghosh · Dept. of Computer Science and Engineering, IIT Kharagpur

Worked numerical problems on SLA availability calculation and service credit computation, followed by Azure SLA terminology and the Cloud Standards Customer Council's 7-step SLA evaluation checklist. The two solved problems are direct exam targets — work through both from scratch.

1 · SLA Refresher
SLA (Service Level Agreement): Formal contract between cloud service provider (CSP) and consumer defining guaranteed service levels (uptime, performance) and remedies for violation.
Metric-based SLA
Parameter-driven
Quantifiable metrics: uptime %, CPU usage, disk throughput, bandwidth. Can be measured and compared against guaranteed values. Most common for IaaS/PaaS.
Policy-based SLA
Rule-driven
Non-quantifiable clauses: where data resides geographically, backup policies, compliance with industry standards (HIPAA, PCI-DSS). Harder to measure but equally binding.
No universal SLA standard yet exists across cloud providers. Each provider defines its own terms; standards are still evolving. The consumer must read provider-specific SLAs carefully.
2 · Problem 1 — SLA Violation Check
Problem statement: A CSP guarantees 99% uptime. An application runs in the cloud for 12 hours/day. At the end of one month (30 days), the total outage was 10.75 hours. Has the provider violated the SLA?

Solution

QuantityCalculationResult
Total service hours12 h/day × 30 days360 hours
Total downtimeGiven10.75 hours
Actual uptime360 − 10.75349.25 hours
Availability %(1 − 10.75/360) × 10096.92%
Guaranteed availability—99%
Conclusion96.92% < 99%SLA VIOLATED
Formula: Availability % = (1 − downtime/total_time) × 100. Compare against the guaranteed %.
Extension: Availability requirements can also vary by time window — e.g., banking SLAs guarantee 99.9% during peak hours (09:00–17:00), 99% during business hours, 97% overnight. Each window is evaluated separately.
3 · Problem 2 — Service Credits & Effective Cost
Problem statement: Company X purchases cloud service from Provider P.
SLA terms: Availability = 99.95%, Service period = 30 days, Max hours/day = 12, Cost = $50/day.
Penalty structure: If availability < 99.95% but ≥ 99% → 10% credit. If availability < 99% → 25% credit.
Actual outages: 5h 30min + 1h 30min + 15min + 2h 25min (total 4 outage events, total downtime = 9h 25min).
Find: Effective cost payable by Company X.

Solution

QuantityCalculationResult
Total service hours12 h/day × 30 days360 hours
Total downtime5h30m + 1h30m + 0h15m + 2h25m9 hours 25 min (≈ 9.417 h)
Availability %(1 − 9.417/360) × 10097.385%
Penalty tier97.385% < 99%25% credit applies
Total cost (contracted)$50 × 30 days$1,500
Service credit25% × $1,500$375
Effective cost payable$1,500 − $375$1,125
Key insight: Even though guaranteed uptime was 99.95%, the actual outage pushed availability below 99%, triggering the higher (25%) penalty tier — not the 10% tier.
Exam trap: First compute availability %; then determine which penalty tier it falls into (the lower the availability, the higher the credit). Don't confuse credit (what consumer gets back) with effective cost (what they actually pay).
4 · Azure SLA Terminology
TermDefinition
Monthly uptime %(Max available minutes − downtime) / max available minutes × 100
DowntimeCumulative minutes during a billing period where the service is unavailable
Error codeHTTP 5xx — indicates operation failed; used to detect and log service failure
Success codeHTTP 2xx — indicates successful operation
External connectivityBidirectional HTTP/HTTPS traffic to/from a public IP address
Availability set2+ VMs deployed across different fault domains — prevents simultaneous failure (eliminates single point of failure)
Fault domainCollection of servers sharing common power and network connectivity — all go down together on power/network failure
Update domainSet of Azure instances to which platform updates are applied concurrently
TenantOne or more role instances deployed in a single package (web role or worker role)
Service creditThe percentage of monthly fees refunded to consumer when SLA is violated
Support windowPeriod during which a specific service feature or version is officially supported
5 · SLA Best Practices (CSCC 7 Steps)
Cloud Standards Customer Council (CSCC) recommends 7 steps when evaluating cloud SLAs:
#StepKey considerations
1Identify cloud actorsNIST 5 actors: Consumer, Provider, Carrier, Broker, Auditor
2Evaluate business-level policiesData residency, backup policy, sub-contracted services, licensing, industry standards (HIPAA, PCI-DSS), excess usage penalties
3Understand service typeIaaS / PaaS / SaaS each have different control variables and measurement complexity
4Identify metricsAvailability %, response time, throughput; frequency of collection; method of measurement
5Consider securityAsset sensitivity, legal/regulatory requirements, geographic data boundary, CSP security capabilities
6Service management requirementsWhat to monitor & report (load, app performance); what to meter (billing); provisioning speed; resource change management
7Prepare for failureRemedy types (service credits, compute hours); liability caps; disaster recovery plan; exit clause for termination by either party
Exit clause is mandatory in every SLA — either party must be able to terminate the relationship with defined obligations and data repatriation terms.
Summary
Availability formula: Availability % = (1 − downtime/total_time) × 100. Compare against guaranteed SLA value to determine violation.
Service credits: Refund percentage applied to total contracted cost when availability falls below SLA threshold. Higher deviation → higher credit tier → lower effective cost paid.
Availability set: Deploy 2+ VMs across different fault domains to eliminate single point of failure. Fault domain = servers sharing power/network (single failure point).
SLA complexity: Real SLAs span metrics (uptime, bandwidth), policies (data residency, licensing), security, and legal clauses — not just uptime calculation.
